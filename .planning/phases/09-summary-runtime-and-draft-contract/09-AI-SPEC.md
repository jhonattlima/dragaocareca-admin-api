## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Content Generation

**Description:**
Backend-owned transcript-to-summary generation for podcast episodes inside `admin-api`. The system reads an already-generated transcript, produces a short Portuguese-BR draft summary, stores it as an editable artifact, and preserves final editorial control in the admin form flow. Good behavior means the output is transcript-faithful, concise, discovery-friendly, and operationally lightweight enough for sequential execution on the target 4 GB VPS.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. The generator invents facts, names, or topics not supported by the transcript.
2. The generator overwrites the final `episodes.summary` field or mutates transcript source artifacts.
3. The generator produces long, vague, or keyword-stuffed output that fails the locked `pt-BR` summary contract.
4. The runtime requires parallel or persistent infrastructure that breaks the low-memory VPS constraint.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** creator media / podcast publishing

**User Population:** podcast operators preparing episode metadata from transcripts inside an internal admin workflow

**Stakes Level:** Medium

**Output Consequence:** generated summaries may be reused as the starting point for public episode metadata, affecting discoverability, editorial quality, and listener expectations

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

Dimension: Listener promise fidelity
Good (domain expert would accept): The summary's main topic, guests, franchises, games, and claimed takeaways are all supported by the transcript, with no invented angle or stronger promise than the episode actually delivers.
Bad (domain expert would flag): The summary introduces unsupported facts, misidentifies a guest or topic, or frames a side mention as the episode's core subject.
Stakes: Critical
Source: Apple Podcasts metadata accuracy guidance; podcast-transcript summarization research on factual inconsistency risk

Dimension: Searchable specificity without spam
Good (domain expert would accept): The summary uses concrete, natural terms listeners would search for, such as guest names, places, games, franchises, or timely topics, when those details are present in the transcript.
Bad (domain expert would flag): The summary stays generic and vague, or it turns into a pile of disconnected keywords written mainly to game search.
Stakes: High
Source: Spotify guidance on episode-description keywords and discoverability; Spotify for Creators spam policy

Dimension: Skimmable episode pitch
Good (domain expert would accept): The text is easy to scan, opens with the main angle, and fits the project's short publish-ready metadata contract without reading like notes or a transcript excerpt.
Bad (domain expert would flag): The text meanders, buries the topic, overexplains the whole conversation, or feels like raw transcript cleanup instead of a description a listener would read before pressing play.
Stakes: High
Source: Spotify creator guidance on concise episode descriptions and teaser-style framing

Dimension: Title-description fit
Good (domain expert would accept): The summary complements the episode title, adds useful context, and avoids repeating the title or spoiling the entire conversation.
Bad (domain expert would flag): The summary duplicates the title, conflicts with it, or gives away the only hook while adding no new value.
Stakes: Medium
Source: Spotify creator guidance on aligning titles and descriptions

Dimension: Spoken-audio robustness
Good (domain expert would accept): Despite disfluencies or rough ASR output, the summary preserves proper nouns and the real meaning of the episode, so an editor would make only light factual cleanup.
Bad (domain expert would flag): Filler chatter, misheard names, or transcript noise dominate the summary and distort what the episode is actually about.
Stakes: High
Source: Apple research on podcast transcript readability and named-entity ASR errors; ACL research on disfluency harming spoken-content summarization

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

- Rare-name drift: guest names, game titles, brands, and franchises are mis-transcribed upstream, then repeated confidently in the summary.
- Banter over-weighting: the model latches onto jokes, filler, or side anecdotes and misses the real editorial angle of the episode.
- Metadata promise inflation: the summary sounds more definitive, newsy, or insightful than the audio actually is, creating a listener-expectation mismatch.
- Search-spam degradation: the summary inserts repetitive topic terms or generic hype language that hurts readability and can look like SEO manipulation rather than editorial metadata.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

No sector-specific regulation was identified for this internal podcast-summary workflow.

- Apple Podcasts requires episode metadata to accurately represent the distributed content, and explicit episodes must still be labeled correctly.
- Apple Podcasts also requires creators to comply with applicable laws in the regions where content is distributed.
- Spotify for Creators treats content made solely to improve SEO as spam, so discoverability language should stay natural and content-faithful.
- Inference from reviewed sources: I did not identify a platform rule requiring disclosure for text-only AI-written episode descriptions in this workflow as of July 23, 2026. Apple does require disclosure when AI is used to generate audio or video content.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** None (custom TypeScript service with a thin provider/CLI adapter)

**Version:** Internal repo implementation; no external orchestration framework

**Rationale:**
Phase 9 is a backend-owned, sequential, one-shot summary job inside an existing Node.js/TypeScript service. The chosen design favors minimal abstraction, a swappable local runtime, and direct reuse of current backend service/file-state patterns instead of adding agent or orchestration framework overhead.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| LangChain (TypeScript) | Adds abstraction and dependency surface before this phase actually needs multi-provider orchestration or richer prompt/eval plumbing |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Partial — runtime should stay swappable even if the initial implementation targets `llama.cpp`-compatible local execution

### Entry Point Pattern
```ts
import { execFile } from "node:child_process";
import { readFile } from "node:fs/promises";
import { promisify } from "node:util";

const execFileAsync = promisify(execFile);

export async function generateSummaryOnce(input: {
  command: string;
  modelPath: string;
  transcriptPath: string;
  contextSize: number;
  maxTokens: number;
}): Promise<string> {
  const transcript = (await readFile(input.transcriptPath, "utf8")).trim();
  if (!transcript) {
    throw new Error("Transcript is empty");
  }

  const prompt = [
    "Voce escreve resumos curtos em pt-BR para episodios de podcast.",
    "Regras: 2-4 frases curtas; abra com o tema principal; cite convidados, franquias, jogos ou temas quando existirem; use termos pesquisaveis naturais; sem hype; sem lista de palavras-chave.",
    "Responda somente com o resumo final.",
    "",
    transcript,
  ].join("\n");

  const { stdout } = await execFileAsync(
    input.command,
    [
      "-m",
      input.modelPath,
      "-c",
      String(input.contextSize),
      "-n",
      String(input.maxTokens),
      "--temp",
      "0.2",
      "--top-p",
      "0.9",
      "--repeat-penalty",
      "1.05",
      "--single-turn",
      "--no-display-prompt",
      "-p",
      prompt,
    ],
    { timeout: 15 * 60 * 1000, maxBuffer: 4 * 1024 * 1024 }
  );

  return stdout.trim();
}
```

### Key Abstractions
| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| `SummaryRuntimeAdapter` | A tiny interface that turns prompt input into generated text or structured JSON, regardless of whether the backend calls `llama-cli`, a hosted SDK, or a future direct provider API | Use as the only model boundary so Phase 10 route/service code stays provider-agnostic |
| `SummaryRuntimeConfig` | Env-backed settings for command, model path, context size, timeout, generation params, and prompt version | Use in `src/config/env.ts`; do not read summary env vars ad hoc inside route handlers |
| `EpisodeDraftState` | Shared `episode.state.json` contract with sibling objects such as `transcript` and `aiSummary` | Use whenever the workflow needs pending/processing/done/error state without touching the final DB summary |
| `SummaryArtifact` | The generated `summary.txt` file stored beside episode draft files | Use as the editable suggestion artifact that later API/frontend phases can return directly |
| `PromptBuilder` | A deterministic function that converts transcript-only input into a versioned prompt contract | Use to keep the writing rules stable and to make retries/cache keys reproducible |

### Common Pitfalls
1. Using `exec()` or `shell: true` with transcript content is the wrong boundary. Node documents that unsanitized input with a shell can trigger command execution; use `execFile()` with an argument array only.
2. Writing `summary.txt` or `episode.state.json` concurrently is unsafe. Node documents that repeated `writeFile()` calls on the same file without awaiting settlement can corrupt data, so keep one in-flight summary job per episode and write artifacts atomically.
3. Assuming every GGUF model will follow your role format without template verification is brittle. Qwen's official examples rely on chat-template application, and `llama.cpp` notes that chat models may need conversation mode or an explicit template when auto-detection is not enough.
4. Oversizing context on the 4 GB VPS is an avoidable operational failure. `llama.cpp` supports long contexts, but this phase should default to a conservative `--ctx-size` and a one-shot CLI process rather than a persistent inference server.
5. Letting generation length float is a bad production default. This workflow needs `2-4` short sentences, so the adapter must set an explicit token cap and reject overlong output instead of trusting model self-control.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**

- Primary runtime target: `Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct` GGUF, default quant `q3_k_m` to fit the current 4 GB VPS expectation; allow `q4_k_m` when the deployed host has proven headroom.
- Default generation parameters: `temperature=0.2`, `top_p=0.9`, `repeat_penalty=1.05`, `max_tokens=180`, `timeout_ms=900000`.
- Default context budget: `context_size=4096` with at least `256` tokens reserved for instructions and output; do not start at 32K just because the base model supports larger windows.
- Required env contract: `EPISODE_SUMMARY_ENABLED`, `EPISODE_SUMMARY_COMMAND`, `EPISODE_SUMMARY_MODEL_PATH`, `EPISODE_SUMMARY_CONTEXT_SIZE`, `EPISODE_SUMMARY_MAX_TOKENS`, `EPISODE_SUMMARY_TIMEOUT_MS`, `EPISODE_SUMMARY_PROMPT_VERSION`.
- Keep model identity and prompt version in state/logs so a regenerated draft can be traced to the exact runtime config that produced it.

**Core Pattern:**

```ts
import { execFile } from "node:child_process";
import { mkdir, readFile, rename, writeFile } from "node:fs/promises";
import path from "node:path";
import { promisify } from "node:util";
import { z } from "zod";

const execFileAsync = promisify(execFile);

const summarySchema = z
  .object({
    summary: z.string().trim().min(80).max(420),
  })
  .superRefine((value, ctx) => {
    const sentences = value.summary
      .split(/[.!?]+/)
      .map((part) => part.trim())
      .filter(Boolean);

    if (sentences.length < 2 || sentences.length > 4) {
      ctx.addIssue({
        code: "custom",
        message: "summary must contain 2-4 sentences",
      });
    }
  });

type SummaryRuntimeConfig = {
  command: string;
  modelPath: string;
  contextSize: number;
  maxTokens: number;
  timeoutMs: number;
  promptVersion: string;
};

const writeTextAtomic = async (filePath: string, text: string): Promise<void> => {
  const tempPath = `${filePath}.tmp`;
  await mkdir(path.dirname(filePath), { recursive: true });
  await writeFile(tempPath, text, "utf8");
  await rename(tempPath, filePath);
};

export async function generateEpisodeDraftSummary(input: {
  transcriptPath: string;
  summaryPath: string;
  config: SummaryRuntimeConfig;
}): Promise<string> {
  const transcript = (await readFile(input.transcriptPath, "utf8")).trim();
  if (!transcript) {
    throw new Error("Cannot generate summary without transcript.txt");
  }

  // Keep the prompt deterministic so retries and cache keys are stable.
  const prompt = [
    "SYSTEM: Voce escreve resumos curtos em pt-BR para episodios de podcast.",
    "SYSTEM: Gere 2-4 frases curtas, comece pelo assunto principal, cite convidados/franquias/jogos/temas quando existirem no transcript e evite hype.",
    "SYSTEM: Responda apenas com JSON no formato {\"summary\": string}.",
    `SYSTEM: prompt_version=${input.config.promptVersion}`,
    "",
    transcript,
  ].join("\n");

  const { stdout } = await execFileAsync(
    input.config.command,
    [
      "-m",
      input.config.modelPath,
      "-c",
      String(input.config.contextSize),
      "-n",
      String(input.config.maxTokens),
      "--temp",
      "0.2",
      "--top-p",
      "0.9",
      "--repeat-penalty",
      "1.05",
      "--single-turn",
      "--no-display-prompt",
      "-p",
      prompt,
    ],
    {
      timeout: input.config.timeoutMs,
      maxBuffer: 4 * 1024 * 1024,
    }
  );

  const parsed = summarySchema.parse(JSON.parse(stdout));
  await writeTextAtomic(input.summaryPath, `${parsed.summary}\n`);
  return parsed.summary;
}
```

The implementation should stay service-first: a protected route or worker triggers the job, `episode-summary.service.ts` owns gating and state changes, and the adapter is the only place that knows whether the runtime is CLI-backed or provider-backed.

**Tool Use:**

- No agent/tool-calling framework is needed in Phase 9. The only execution boundary is the local runtime adapter.
- Keep the adapter interface narrow: `generate(prompt, options) -> rawText`.
- Use `execFile()` argument arrays only. Never interpolate transcript text into a shell command.
- Keep tracing lightweight for now: log `episodeId`, `stateVersion`, `modelId`, `promptVersion`, transcript byte length, duration, retry count, and validation failures. Do not log the entire transcript by default.
- No vector DB, embeddings, or semantic search library belongs in this phase; the summary is transcript-only and sequential.

**State Management:**

- Replace `transcript.state.json` as the primary contract with `episode.state.json`.
- Store sibling step objects, not separate files, for example:
  - `transcript.status`, `transcript.updatedAt`
  - `aiSummary.status`, `aiSummary.updatedAt`, `aiSummary.promptVersion`, `aiSummary.model`, `aiSummary.error`
- Increment a shared `version` on every new summary attempt so stale async completions cannot overwrite a newer request.
- Persist `summary.txt` as the generated suggestion artifact. Never auto-write `episodes.summary`.
- Keep `transcript.txt` immutable from the summary workflow; it is read-only source input.

**Context Window Strategy:**

- Start with a one-pass path when the normalized transcript fits inside `context_size - reserved_prompt_tokens - reserved_output_tokens`.
- Normalize before token budgeting: trim repeated blank lines, obvious non-content markers, and duplicated lines, mirroring the existing transcription cleanup style.
- If the transcript is too large, stay sequential and transcript-only:
  - First pass: split the transcript into deterministic windows and generate terse notes per window.
  - Second pass: synthesize the final `2-4` sentence summary from those notes.
- Record when compaction occurred in `aiSummary` state so later review can distinguish full-context runs from reduced-context runs.
- If compaction still cannot fit within the configured timeout/memory budget, fail explicitly with `aiSummary.status="error"` instead of silently dropping large transcript regions.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> Written by `gsd-ai-researcher`. Cross-cutting patterns every developer building AI systems needs — independent of framework choice.

### Structured Outputs with Pydantic

This repo is TypeScript-first, so use `zod` as the practical equivalent of Pydantic at the adapter boundary. The model should return a small JSON envelope, the service validates it, and only the validated `summary` string is persisted to `summary.txt`.

```ts
import { z } from "zod";

export const summaryEnvelopeSchema = z
  .object({
    summary: z.string().trim().min(80).max(420),
    searchableTerms: z.array(z.string().trim().min(2)).min(1).max(3),
  })
  .superRefine((value, ctx) => {
    const sentences = value.summary
      .split(/[.!?]+/)
      .map((part) => part.trim())
      .filter(Boolean);

    if (sentences.length < 2 || sentences.length > 4) {
      ctx.addIssue({
        code: "custom",
        message: "summary must contain 2-4 sentences",
      });
    }
  });
```

Integration pattern for this custom service:

- CLI adapter: ask for JSON, `JSON.parse(stdout)`, then `summaryEnvelopeSchema.safeParse(...)`.
- Hosted provider adapter later: keep the same schema and adapter contract; if a provider supports native structured outputs, map that feature into the same validated envelope before the service sees it.
- Persist only the validated `summary`; keep `searchableTerms` in logs or `episode.state.json` if later review needs them.

Retry policy:

- Allow `2` retries after the first failed attempt, for `3` total attempts.
- Retry only on parse/validation failure, transient process timeout, or empty output.
- Log `episodeId`, `attempt`, `modelId`, `promptVersion`, transcript hash, output byte size, and validation error messages.
- Surface the failure after the final attempt by setting `aiSummary.status="error"` and preserving the last valid `summary.txt` if one already exists.

### Async-First Design

Use `fs/promises`, `promisify(execFile)`, and an async service API end to end. This keeps the background job composable with existing Express handlers and avoids blocking the event loop during file I/O.

The common mistake in this stack is not event-loop nesting, but concurrent writes to the same artifact. Do not `Promise.all()` multiple writes to `summary.txt` or `episode.state.json`, and do not allow a second summary job to start for the same episode while one is already `pending` or `processing`.

Prefer `await` over streaming for this phase. A complete output is required before JSON validation, sentence-count enforcement, and atomic file replacement. Stream only in a later UX phase if the admin UI needs live progress, and even then keep a buffered full response for final validation.

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

Keep system instructions separate from transcript content. In practice:

- System prompt: checked-in, versioned rules for `pt-BR`, `2-4` short sentences, discovery-friendly language, and explicit anti-hype constraints.
- User payload: the transcript only, fenced as untrusted content to summarize rather than instructions to obey.
- Few-shot examples: keep them inline only if they are short and stable. Do not introduce dynamic retrieval for examples in this phase because the locked contract is transcript-only input.
- Set `max_tokens` explicitly on every call. Never leave the generator unbounded in production just because the local runtime can continue emitting text.

### Context Window Management

This is not a RAG system, so context control is a transcript compaction problem:

- Estimate prompt size before invocation and reserve output budget up front.
- Prefer full-transcript one-pass generation when it fits.
- When it does not fit, use deterministic sequential map-reduce inside the same job: window notes first, final summary second.
- Record `inputTruncated` or `compacted` state flags so operators know whether the summary saw the full source text.
- Never silently chop the transcript tail and pretend the run was complete; that is how guest names and late-episode topics disappear.

### Cost and Latency Budget

The initial local `llama.cpp` path has no per-token vendor charge, so the budget is CPU time, RAM, and operator overhead rather than API spend.

- Target budget per episode: one sequential summary job, normally one model call, with a hard timeout of `15` minutes and a preferred steady-state latency under `90` seconds after transcript normalization.
- Exact-match cache: key by transcript file hash + prompt version + model identifier + generation params. If the transcript and prompt did not change, return the existing `summary.txt` instead of regenerating it.
- Semantic cache: optional and low priority here. It is only worth adding later for oversized-transcript intermediate notes, not for final episode summaries.
- Cheaper-model routing: if a fallback two-pass compaction path is needed, a smaller local model can generate intermediate window notes while the primary `3B` instruct model remains responsible for the final outward-facing summary.

### Reference Dataset

**Size:** Start with `12` labeled examples during Phase 9 implementation; expand to `20` before Phase 10/11 rollout.

**Composition:**
- Clean happy paths with obvious main topics and correct guest/franchise/game names.
- Noisy ASR paths with disfluencies, duplicated lines, or partially corrupted proper nouns.
- Long transcripts that trigger deterministic compaction/two-pass summarization.
- Banter-heavy or side-anecdote-heavy episodes that tempt promise inflation.
- Title-fit cases where the title is teaser-like and the summary must add context without repetition.
- Negative controls for empty/missing transcript prerequisites and stale-state/write-protection behavior.

**Labeling:**
- Senior podcast editor labels each example as `publish-ready` or `revise` and tags the failed dimensions when it does not pass.
- Show producer/host reviewer signs off on examples involving guest identity, inside references, or proper-noun ambiguity.
- Code-based labels cover sentence count, character budget, artifact isolation, retry behavior, and transcript prerequisite enforcement.
- LLM Judge pre-scores fidelity/style only after calibration against the human labels; disagreements are added to the human-review queue.
- Create the first `10` examples before merging the runtime service, not after; add the remaining production examples while implementing the protected API exposure in later phases.

|-----------|---------|--------------|
| Transcript prerequisite gate | `transcript.txt` is missing/empty, or transcript state is not ready | Block generation, set `aiSummary.status="error"` with a prerequisite message, and keep prior draft artifacts untouched. |
| Output contract validator | Model output fails JSON/Zod validation, falls outside `2-4` sentences / character budget, fails `pt-BR` heuristic, or trips repetition/keyword-spam thresholds | Retry up to `2` additional times; on final failure, block persistence of the new draft and preserve the last valid `summary.txt`. |
| Artifact isolation + stale-write guard | The run attempts to mutate `transcript.txt` or `episodes.summary`, or `episode.state.json.version` changed while the job was in flight | Block the write, preserve existing artifacts, and flag the run as stale/conflicting for operator review. |
| Sequential runtime lock | The same episode already has a `pending`/`processing` summary job, or the runtime would exceed the single-job-per-episode contract | Block duplicate starts and emit an operations alert instead of queueing uncontrolled parallel work. |
| Named-entity drift flag | The summary introduces entity-like tokens, numbers, or dates not found in the normalized transcript, or compaction ran on a transcript with low-confidence entity coverage | Persist the draft as review-required and flag it for human validation before it is treated as publish-ready. |

### Offline (Flywheel)

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| Listener promise fidelity | Review `100%` of named-entity drift flags plus a `25%` random weekly sample of clean runs | If pass rate drops below `90%` on the latest `20` reviewed runs, revise the prompt/compaction strategy and require editor review on all drafts until recovered. |
| Searchable specificity without spam | Weekly `20%` sample, weighted toward summaries with repeated top terms or unusually dense noun phrases | If spam/generic failures exceed `10%`, tighten repetition validators and refresh prompt examples. |
| Spoken-audio robustness | All noisy-transcript fixtures in CI plus at least `10` production runs per week with high transcript-cleanup ratios | If failure rate exceeds `15%`, adjust transcript normalization and add more noisy examples before changing the model. |
| Title-description fit | `10%` sample of episodes once a title is available, with extra review for teaser-style titles | If duplication/conflict exceeds `15%`, add a title-aware offline eval and revise the summary rubric/examples. |
| Runtime budget adherence | `100%` of runs via trace telemetry for latency, retries, timeouts, and compaction rate | If `p95` latency exceeds `90s`, timeout rate exceeds `5%`, or compaction rate exceeds `30%`, revisit context size/model choice before widening rollout. |

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** `Arize Phoenix` via OpenTelemetry spans emitted from the summary service and runtime adapter. `Promptfoo` remains the regression tool in CI, not the production tracer.

**Key Metrics to Track:**
- `summary_generation_success_rate`
- `first_pass_validation_failure_rate`
- `summary_generation_latency_ms` (`p50`, `p95`)
- `summary_compaction_rate`
- `named_entity_drift_flag_rate`
- `sampled_fidelity_pass_rate` from the offline review batch

**Alert Thresholds:**
- Page if `summary_generation_success_rate < 95%` over a rolling `24h` window or if `3` consecutive runs fail for the same failure class.
- Investigate if `first_pass_validation_failure_rate > 10%` over the last `50` runs.
- Investigate if `p95(summary_generation_latency_ms) > 90000` for two consecutive daily windows or if any run hits the `15` minute timeout.
- Investigate if `named_entity_drift_flag_rate > 15%` or `summary_compaction_rate > 30%` over the last `50` runs.
- Block wider rollout if `sampled_fidelity_pass_rate < 90%` on the latest reviewed batch or if any critical safety/compliance audit fails.

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**
- Always sample `100%` of runs with retries, compaction, named-entity drift flags, stale-write conflicts, or timeout/validation failures.
- Randomly sample `20%` of otherwise clean runs each week to catch silent quality drift.
- Once the protected summary endpoint is live, add `100%` sampling for drafts that editors fully discard or heavily rewrite.
- Store transcript hash, title, summary, model/prompt version, and issue tags with each sample; reviewers can fetch the underlying transcript from episode artifacts when needed, instead of duplicating raw transcript text into telemetry.
